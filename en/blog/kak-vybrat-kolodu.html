How to choose your first tarot deck

6 October 2026

Your first deck is not about aesthetics. It is about which system you will find easiest to learn from. Let us take it in order.

Three systems worth knowing

  • Rider–Waite–Smith (RWS). The gold standard: all 78 cards carry a drawn scene, so a card can be read even without a textbook. This is the deck used on this site.
  • Tarot de Marseille. The oldest of the living systems. Its Minor Arcana are just rows of staves and cups, with no scenes. Harder to read: you need numerology and the elements.
  • Thoth Tarot (Crowley). Tightly bound to astrology and kabbalah. For experienced readers: without preparation the cards will stay silent.

There are also oracles, which are not tarot: any number of cards, no strict structure. They are gentler and simpler, but you will not learn classical reading from them.

What actually matters for a beginner

  • 78 cards with scenes on the Minor Arcana. That saves you months of study.
  • A size and print quality that suit you. The cards should sit easily in your hand and not delaminate after a month.
  • No “built-in” interpretation printed on the card. Labels like “happiness” or “betrayal” stop you thinking for yourself.
  • Plenty of available descriptions. If there is a lot of commentary and literature on the deck, learning is easier.

What not to do

  • Buying “the prettiest one” first. Art decks are often far from classical meanings: you will be reading the artist's fantasy, not tarot.
  • Chasing an expensive edition. A normal edition with good printing is fine for learning.
  • Believing “this deck does not work for me”. More often it means “I do not understand the system”. Take the Waite and work it out.
  • Buying five decks at once. Mixing systems in a beginner's head is a reliable way to get lost.

How long learning takes

  • The basics: how the deck is built (Major Arcana, suits, numbers, court cards) — two or three evenings of reading.
  • The meanings of 78 cards — a month or two of daily practice, one card a day.
  • Reading a whole spread — three to six months of regularity.

A useful habit: draw one card each day and write down in the evening how it showed up. In a month you will have personal experience that no textbook can replace.

Do you have to “charge” the deck and keep it in a pouch

That is a question of tradition, not of effectiveness. If the ritual helps you concentrate, do it. If not, the cards need only a dry place and a pouch to keep them from wearing.

Begin with the <a href="/taro/en/">card of the day</a>: it is the simplest practice, and it will show you at once whether you enjoy working with the deck.
