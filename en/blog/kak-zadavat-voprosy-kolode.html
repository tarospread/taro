How to ask the tarot deck a question

7 October 2026

This is the core skill in working with tarot. The deck is like a wise conversation partner: ask “how are you?” and you get a general sigh; ask “what is stopping me from finishing this report?” and you get concrete advice.

Rule one: an open shape

The cards work in images and processes; they need room to answer. Questions that can be answered “yes” or “no” almost always produce a blurred result.

  • Weak: “Will I get married this year?”
  • Strong: “What do I need to understand about my relationship right now?”
  • Strong: “What will help me meet a partner for a serious relationship?”

Rule two: focus on yourself

You do not control other people, only yourself. Questions about third parties return a projection of your own fears, not someone else's thoughts.

  • Weak: “What does he think of me?”
  • Strong: “What can I do to make the relationship warmer?”

The exception is when the question is about how you interact with someone: “how do I set boundaries with a colleague?” is a fair question.

Rule three: no fatalism

The position “what will happen to me” turns you into a spectator of your own life. “What are my resources and what can I do” gives the controls back.

  • Weak: “Will he leave me?”, “Will I be fired?”
  • Strong: “What can I learn from this difficult patch?”, “How can I show my value at work so that I am appreciated?”

Rule four: time frame and scale

If you ask about the future, name the horizon. “What awaits me?” is too vague; the cards will show either the distant past or the distant future.

  • Strong: “Which events will matter for me in the next three months?”

And do not stuff love, money, work and health into one question. One question — one spread.

Reframing: from problem to solution

Dead-end questionQuestion that works
Why do I have no money?What should I change in my approach to money so that income appears?
Does he love me?What can I do to strengthen our relationship?
Will I be fired?How can I show my value at work and keep my position?
What happened in the past?How do past events affect my situation now, and what should I do about it?

How to ask, practically

  • Say the question aloud, or state it clearly to yourself, before you shuffle the cards.
  • Keep it in mind while you lay the cards out — do not reach for your phone.
  • One question — one spread. Asking again “until you like the answer” is pointless: the answer will not get “better”, you will simply stop trusting it.

Ethics

  • Do not ask about anything that overrides someone else's free will: “how do I make him come back” does not work and is not ethical.
  • Tarot does not diagnose and does not give legal advice. Ask differently: “what should I pay attention to in my health?”, “what risks are there in my case?”

Ready to try? Phrase an open question and do a three-card spread — and before that you can draw a card of the day.
