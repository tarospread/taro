The Celtic Cross: a 10-card spread, position by position

4 October 2026

The Celtic Cross is the best-known large tarot spread. Ten cards, two figures: a cross and a staff. It is good when a situation is tangled and you need not a quick answer but a full picture.

The layout

```

[3]

[6]

[5] [1+2] [4]

[8]

[10]

[7]

[9]

```

Card 2 is laid across the first — that is the crossing, the central conflict of the reading.

What the positions mean

  • 1 — Situation. What is at the centre of attention now.
  • 2 — Obstacle. What crosses the situation and gets in the way.
  • 3 — Foundation. The past, the ground everything grew from.
  • 4 — Recent past. What is leaving, losing its power.
  • 5 — Possibility. The best scenario, what stands above the situation.
  • 6 — Near future. What is approaching.
  • 7 — Your position. How you yourself relate to the situation.
  • 8 — Environment. The influence of other people and circumstances.
  • 9 — Hopes and fears. The main answer often hides here.
  • 10 — Outcome. Where it all leads if nothing changes.

The order of reading

  • First the pair 1+2: the essence and the conflict. Without it the rest does not read.
  • Then the vertical 3–6–10: where it came from, what is approaching, where it leads.
  • Then the horizontal 5–4: what helps and what is leaving.
  • Finally 7–8–9: you, your surroundings, your expectations and fears.

This order matters: a beginner who reads the cards by number, one after another, loses the logic of the spread.

What to look at overall

  • Four or more Major Arcana — the situation really is a turning point.
  • Position 9 often contradicts position 10: that means the person fears what is actually approaching, or else expects something that will not happen.
  • Many reversed cards — the spread speaks of inner resistance rather than outer events.

When you do not need the Celtic Cross

  • For a household question like “should I go to this meeting”, three cards are enough.
  • It is too heavy for everyday practice.
  • Do not do it twice in one day on the same question: the answer will not get clearer, it will get muddier.

For a beginner it is easier to start with a three-card spread and move on to the Cross once the card meanings come to mind without a prompt.
