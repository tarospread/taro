Tarot and psychology: why the cards work even without mysticism

26 September 2026

You can disbelieve in predictions and still get something out of Tarot. Psychology explains this without any mysticism.

Jung's archetypes

Carl Gustav Jung believed that culture carries universal images — archetypes living in the collective unconscious: the Mother, the Sage, the Shadow, the Hero. The Major Arcana of Tarot fit that list with suspicious ease. Jung used cards in therapy as a way to start a conversation with a patient's inner material.

Projection: the card as a screen

When a person looks at an ambiguous image, they inevitably complete it with their own experience. In psychology this is called projection. A tarot card is an ideal screen of that kind: concrete enough to hold on to, abstract enough for everyone to see their own thing.

That is why the same card means different things to two people — and both will be right.

The Barnum effect

Statements like “you are capable of more, but sometimes you doubt yourself” feel accurate to almost everyone. This is the Barnum effect — the tendency to take general descriptions as personal. The good news: even knowing about the effect, we keep using it. The bad news: unscrupulous “fortune tellers” lean on it heavily.

Why a spread is still useful

  • It forces you to put into words the question a person usually avoids.
  • It gives structure: the positions of a spread are ready-made angles for thinking.
  • It creates a pause: instead of an immediate reaction there is time to think.
  • It returns authorship: “what can I do” is a stronger question than “what will happen to me”.

What should raise a flag

  • Intimidation: “someone has cursed you, you must have it removed urgently”.
  • Exact dates of death, illness, disaster.
  • Demanding money to “remove” a threat that was just announced.
  • Claiming that without a tarot reader you cannot cope on your own.

We follow one simple rule: the cards are a tool for reflection. You read the spread and decide yourself what to do with it. No “fatal” wording, no scare tactics.

For a conversation with yourself, a short four-card spread works well — Mirror: how you are seen, what you hide, where the resource is and where the growth area is.
