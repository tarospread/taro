Reversed cards: do you need them at all

27 September 2026

Reversed cards are the most disputed topic in Tarot. Some readers refuse to use them on principle, others consider them half of the system.

Where they came from

The idea itself is simple: turn a card over and you get its “shadow” side. Upright — the energy is there and works; reversed — the same energy is blocked, inverted or turned inward.

An example: “the Sun” upright is joy and success; reversed it is the same joy, but postponed or not yet noticed.

Three ways to read a reversed card

  • Blockage: what should work does not work.
  • Excess: too much of a quality — for instance, caution has turned into cowardice.
  • Turning inward: the energy is aimed at inner work rather than outer events.

Arguments against

  • Reversals double the amount to memorise: 78 meanings become 156.
  • Many modern readers read upright cards only — and that is not a simplification but a deliberate system.
  • A reversed card often simply reflects the mood of the person asking, not the situation.

What a beginner should do

  • For the first months read upright cards only — the base forms faster that way.
  • When confidence appears, switch reversals on and watch whether the meaning of the spread changes.
  • Do not fear the reversed “scary” cards: they almost always mean a softening, not an intensification.

In our spreads reversed cards are off by default — but you can turn them on with one switch if that suits you better.
